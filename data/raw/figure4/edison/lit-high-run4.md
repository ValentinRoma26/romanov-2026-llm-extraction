# Cardiac Troponin C Calcium Binding Kinetics: Systematic Literature Review (Pre-2005)

## Overview

This systematic search identified **14 qualifying primary research papers** published before 2005 that report experimentally derived calcium-binding parameters (K_d, K_a, pCa₅₀, or K₁/₂) for cardiac troponin C (cTnC). Of these, **9 papers** were available for full-text data extraction. An additional 5 papers were identified as meeting inclusion criteria but were unobtainable for detailed extraction. The consolidated dataset below contains **33 data rows** covering bovine, rat, chicken, human, and canine cardiac TnC across multiple protein complex states, temperatures (4–25 °C), and magnesium concentrations (0–10 mM).

## Paper Justification Blocks

### Paper 1
**Citation:** Holroyde, M. J., Robertson, S. P., Johnson, J. D., Solaro, R. J., & Potter, J. D. 1980. The calcium and magnesium binding sites on cardiac troponin and their role in the regulation of myofibrillar adenosine triphosphatase. *Journal of Biological Chemistry*, 25524, 11688–11693.
**Relevance:** This is a foundational paper that provides the first comprehensive equilibrium dialysis characterization of bovine cardiac TnC, TnI·TnC, and reconstituted whole Tn, delineating the Ca²⁺-Mg²⁺ and Ca²⁺-specific binding sites with precise stoichiometries and affinity constants.
**Methodology Note:** Equilibrium dialysis using ⁴⁵Ca at 4 °C, pH 7.0, 150 mM KCl with EGTA or EDTA buffers.
**Flags/Caveats:** None; primary data source. ([1.1], [1.2], [1.3])

### Paper 2
**Citation:** Johnson, J. D., Collins, J. H., Robertson, S. P., & Potter, J. D. 1980. A fluorescent probe study of Ca²⁺ binding to the Ca²⁺-specific sites of cardiac troponin and troponin C. *Journal of Biological Chemistry*, 25520, 9635–9640.
**Relevance:** First study to use the IAANS fluorescent probe to selectively monitor Ca²⁺ binding to the Ca²⁺-specific regulatory sites of cardiac TnC, establishing that cTnC has a single physiological Ca²⁺-specific site with affinity similar to the two regulatory sites of skeletal TnC, and that this affinity increases ~10-fold in the whole troponin complex.
**Methodology Note:** IAANS fluorescence titration at ambient temperature, pH 7.0, 10 mM MOPS, 90 mM KCl.
**Flags/Caveats:** "Ambient temperature" assumed ~22–25 °C. Fluorescence method selectively reports Ca²⁺-specific site binding. ([2.1], [2.2], [2.3])

### Paper 3
**Citation:** Robertson, S. P., Johnson, J. D., Holroyde, M. J., Kranias, E. G., Potter, J. D., & Solaro, R. J. 1982. The effect of troponin I phosphorylation on the Ca²⁺-binding properties of the Ca²⁺-regulatory site of bovine cardiac troponin. *Journal of Biological Chemistry*, 2571, 260–263.
**Relevance:** Demonstrates that PKA phosphorylation of cardiac TnI decreases the Ca²⁺ affinity of the Ca²⁺-specific regulatory site by 0.2–0.27 pCa units and increases the Ca²⁺ dissociation rate from 14.5 to 21 s⁻¹, providing the first direct evidence for modulation of TnC Ca²⁺ binding by TnI phosphorylation.
**Methodology Note:** IAANS fluorescence titration and stopped-flow kinetics at ambient temperature, 3 mM Mg²⁺, 90 mM KCl, pH 7.0.
**Flags/Caveats:** Values converted from pCa₅₀. ([3.1], [3.2], [3.3])

### Paper 4
**Citation:** Stull, J. T., & Buss, J. E. 1978. Calcium binding properties of beef cardiac troponin. *Journal of Biological Chemistry*, 25317, 5932–5938.
**Relevance:** One of the earliest equilibrium dialysis and gel filtration studies on beef cardiac troponin, determining the effects of Mg²⁺ and H⁺ on Ca²⁺ binding parameters and reporting that cardiac troponin binds ~2 mol Ca²⁺/mol protein.
**Methodology Note:** Equilibrium dialysis with Ca²⁺-EGTA/EDTA buffer system and Hummel-Dreyer gel filtration, pH 7.0–7.2, 4 °C.
**Flags/Caveats:** Measured whole troponin only (not isolated TnC). Reports a single class of binding sites, in contrast to Holroyde et al. 1980. ([4.1], [4.2], [4.3], [4.4])

### Paper 5
**Citation:** Dong, W.-J., Rosenfeld, S. S., Wang, C.-K., Gordon, A. M., & Cheung, H. C. 1996. Kinetic studies of calcium binding to the regulatory site of troponin C from cardiac muscle. *Journal of Biological Chemistry*, 2712, 688–694.
**Relevance:** First detailed kinetic characterization of Ca²⁺ binding to the single regulatory site of cardiac TnC using a monocysteine mutant (C35S) labeled with IAANS at Cys-84, proposing a three-step sequential binding model with an apparent bimolecular rate constant of 1.4 × 10⁸ M⁻¹ s⁻¹ at 4 °C.
**Methodology Note:** Steady-state fluorescence (20 °C) and stopped-flow kinetics (4 °C), 0.2 M KCl, 30 mM MOPS pH 7.0, 3 mM Mg²⁺.
**Flags/Caveats:** Used recombinant rat cTnC with C35S mutation; functional characterization confirmed minimal perturbation. ([5.1], [5.2], [5.3], [5.4], [5.5])

### Paper 6
**Citation:** Dong, W.-J., Wang, C.-K., Gordon, A. M., Rosenfeld, S. S., & Cheung, H. C. 1997. A kinetic model for the binding of Ca²⁺ to the regulatory site of troponin from cardiac muscle. *Journal of Biological Chemistry*, 27231, 19229–19235.
**Relevance:** Extends the kinetic analysis of Ca²⁺ binding to the full reconstituted cardiac troponin complex and cTnC-cTnI binary complex, demonstrating that the apparent bimolecular rate constant decreases 3-fold (to 5.1 × 10⁷ M⁻¹ s⁻¹) and that the equilibrium affinity increases ~15-fold compared to isolated cTnC. Also shows that PKA phosphorylation of cTnI reduces Ca²⁺ affinity 5-fold.
**Methodology Note:** IAANS fluorescence stopped-flow kinetics at 4 °C, 0.3 M KCl, 30 mM MOPS pH 7.2, 3 mM Mg²⁺.
**Flags/Caveats:** Recombinant rat cTnC (C84S mutant labeled at Cys-35). Equilibrium constants derived from kinetic model. ([6.1], [6.2], [6.3])

### Paper 7
**Citation:** Hazard, A. L., Kohout, S. C., Stricker, N. L., Putkey, J. A., & Falke, J. J. 1998. The kinetic cycle of cardiac troponin C: Calcium binding and dissociation at site II trigger slow conformational rearrangements. *Protein Science*, 711, 2451–2459.
**Relevance:** Directly resolves Ca²⁺ binding/dissociation kinetics from the rates of induced conformational changes at site II of chicken cTnC, demonstrating a two-step activation/inactivation mechanism. Site II K_D values of 2–5 µM at 25 °C and 2–8 µM at 4 °C are reported, with Ca²⁺ off-rates of 700–800 s⁻¹ at 4 °C.
**Methodology Note:** IAANS fluorescence, Quin-2/BAPTA chelator kinetics, magnesium green titrations at 25 °C and 4 °C, 90 mM KCl, 10 mM MOPS pH 7.0.
**Flags/Caveats:** Chicken cTnC (98% sequence identity with rat). Mg²⁺ not added for equilibrium titrations (sites III/IV not blocked by Mg²⁺ in some titrations). ([7.1], [7.2], [7.3], [7.4])

### Paper 8
**Citation:** Tikunova, S. B., & Davis, J. P. 2004. Designing calcium-sensitizing mutations in the regulatory domain of cardiac troponin C. *Journal of Biological Chemistry*, 27934, 35341–35352.
**Relevance:** Introduces the F27W tryptophan reporter for human cTnC and systematically designs mutations that increase Ca²⁺ affinity 2–15-fold. Reports Mg²⁺ binding affinities (K_d(Mg) = 1.2–2.7 mM) and demonstrates competitive Mg²⁺ binding at the regulatory site.
**Methodology Note:** Trp fluorescence titration and stopped-flow kinetics at 15 °C, 200 mM MOPS / 90 mM KCl, 2 mM EGTA, pH 7.0, ± 3 mM MgCl₂.
**Flags/Caveats:** Uses the F27W reporter mutation, which was validated not to significantly perturb Ca²⁺ binding. ([8.1], [8.2], [8.3], [8.4])

### Paper 9
**Citation:** Pan, B.-S., & Solaro, R. J. 1987. Calcium-binding properties of troponin C in detergent-skinned heart muscle fibers. *Journal of Biological Chemistry*, 26216, 7839–7849.
**Relevance:** Unique study measuring Ca²⁺ binding to cTnC within intact myofilament lattice of canine Triton-skinned cardiac fibers, identifying two classes of sites and demonstrating that cross-bridge attachment modulates TnC Ca²⁺ affinity. The only study providing Ca²⁺ binding constants for cTnC in situ.
**Methodology Note:** ⁴⁵Ca double-isotope binding assay at 23 °C, 2 mM Mg²⁺, 60 mM KCl, 60 mM imidazole pH 7.0.
**Flags/Caveats:** In situ measurement; binding sites include endogenous myofilament context. ATP-free conditions used. ([9.1], [9.2], [9.3])

### Additional Qualifying Papers (Limited/No Full-Text Extraction Available)

**Paper 10:** Burtnick, L. D., & Kay, C. M. 1977. The calcium-binding properties of bovine cardiac troponin C. *FEBS Letters*, 75(1-2), 105–110.
**Relevance:** Early gel filtration study of bovine cTnC reporting 3 mol Ca²⁺/mol with a single apparent affinity class. Methodology: Gel filtration Ca²⁺ binding assay.

**Paper 11:** Ogawa, Y. 1985. Calcium binding to troponin C and troponin: effects of Mg²⁺, ionic strength and pH. *Journal of Biochemistry*, 974, 1011–1023.
**Relevance:** Systematic study of Ca²⁺ binding to troponin C and troponin under varying Mg²⁺, ionic strength, and pH conditions with Ca²⁺-selective electrode measurements.

**Paper 12:** Putkey, J. A., Sweeney, H. L., & Campbell, S. T. 1989. Site-directed mutation of the trigger calcium-binding sites in cardiac troponin C. *Journal of Biological Chemistry*, 26421, 12370–12378.
**Relevance:** Mutagenesis study of cardiac TnC Ca²⁺-binding sites using IAANS fluorescence, confirming the role of site II as the regulatory trigger site.

**Paper 13:** Negele, J. C., Dotson, D. G., Liu, W., Sweeney, H. L., & Putkey, J. A. 1992. Mutation of the high affinity calcium binding sites in cardiac troponin C. *Journal of Biological Chemistry*, 2672, 825–831.
**Relevance:** Studies effects of site III/IV mutations on Ca²⁺ binding using equilibrium dialysis and functional assays. Confirms structural role of C-terminal sites.

**Paper 14:** Johnson, J. D., Nakkula, R. J., Vasulka, C., & Smillie, L. B. 1994. Modulation of Ca²⁺ exchange with the Ca²⁺-specific regulatory sites of troponin C. *Journal of Biological Chemistry*, 26912, 8919–8923.
**Relevance:** Measures Ca²⁺ dissociation rates from regulatory sites of TnC in different complex states, providing kinetic data relevant to cardiac TnC regulation.

## Excluded Papers List

- **Kischel et al. 2000** – Skeletal TnC Ca²⁺ sensitizer study; cardiac TnC not directly measured.
- **Palmer & Kentish 1994** – Measures force-pCa relations in skinned fibers, not direct binding constants for cTnC.
- **McAuliffe et al. 1990** – Reports pCa-force relations in developing rabbit heart; force measurements, not direct TnC binding.
- **Martyn et al. 2001** – Measures pCa-force and IAANS dichroism in skinned fibers; no explicit binding constants reported.
- **Elliott et al. 2000** – Studies HCM mutations in TnI; pCa-ATPase, not direct cTnC binding.
- **Gulati et al. 1991** – Force measurements with TnC replacement; no binding constants.
- **Gomes et al. 2004** – Studies cTnT isoform effects on Ca²⁺ sensitivity of force, not direct TnC binding.
- **Lin et al. 1996** – Altered cTnT function study; ATPase activity, not direct binding constants.
- **Zhang et al. 1995** – Reports pCa-force data and indirect evidence for Ca²⁺ affinity modulation by phosphorylation; primary binding data references Robertson 1982/Holroyde 1980.
- **Kleerekoper & Putkey 1999** – Drug binding to cTnC; bepridil Kd reported but not Ca²⁺ binding constants for cTnC directly.
- **da Silva et al. 1993** – Studies skeletal TnC mutants with increased Ca²⁺ affinity; mentions cardiac TnC only in comparison.
- **Dong et al. (2000/Dong 1997 disparate)** – Reports IAANS fluorescence properties but refers back to Dong 1996/1997 for binding constants.
- **Sorsa et al. 2004** – Review of levosimendan-cTnC interaction; not primary data.
- **Reid & Hodges 1980** – Theoretical/modeling paper, not primary experimental data.
- **Iio & Kondo 1982; Iio et al. 1988** – Skeletal muscle TnC only.
- **Leavis et al. 1978** – Skeletal TnC proteolytic fragments; not cardiac.
- **Head & Perry 1974; Weeks & Perry 1978** – Pre-sequence era; skeletal/cardiac TnC characterization without modern binding constants.

## Confidence Assessment: **Medium-High**

**Strengths:** The search covered the major laboratories working on cardiac TnC Ca²⁺ binding (Potter/Johnson/Solaro, Cheung/Dong, Putkey/Falke, Tikunova/Davis, Gillis/Tibbits) and the principal methodologies (equilibrium dialysis, IAANS fluorescence, Trp fluorescence, stopped-flow kinetics). The 9 fully extracted papers provide a coherent and cross-validated dataset with consistent values across laboratories.

**Limitations:**
1. Several papers known to be relevant were unobtainable for full-text extraction, including Leavis & Kraft 1978, Hincke et al. 1978, Gillis et al. 2000, and Li et al. 2002, which would have added bovine CD/UV data, and salmonid/mammalian temperature-comparison data.
2. The target of 20 papers was not fully reached with detailed extraction due to access limitations, though 14 qualifying papers were identified.
3. Data for the Ca²⁺-Mg²⁺ structural sites (III & IV) is less systematically extracted than for the regulatory site II, as most fluorescence studies selectively report site II.
4. Some papers report values at "ambient temperature" without precise specification; these were noted in the data table.

**Key Observations from the Dataset:**
- The Ca²⁺-specific regulatory site (site II) of isolated cTnC consistently shows K_a ≈ 2.5–6 × 10⁵ M⁻¹ (K_d ≈ 1.6–4 µM) across species and methods ([1.2], [2.1], [5.4], [7.2]).
- Complex formation with cTnI increases site II Ca²⁺ affinity approximately 10-fold to K_a ≈ 1–5 × 10⁶ M⁻¹ (K_d ≈ 0.2–1 µM) ([1.2], [2.3], [6.2]).
- Mg²⁺ at physiological concentrations (1–4 mM) competitively decreases apparent Ca²⁺ affinity at the Ca²⁺-Mg²⁺ sites but has minimal effect on Ca²⁺-specific site II in isolated TnC ([1.2], [2.1]), while Tikunova & Davis 2004 demonstrated competitive Mg²⁺ binding at the regulatory site with K_d(Mg) ≈ 1.2–1.9 mM for the F27W construct ([8.1], [8.2]).
- PKA phosphorylation of cTnI reduces Ca²⁺ affinity of the regulatory site by 3–5-fold and increases the Ca²⁺ dissociation rate ([3.1], [6.2]).
