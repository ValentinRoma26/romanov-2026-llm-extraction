# Cardiac Troponin C Calcium-Binding Kinetics Before 2005

## Scope and screening

I screened pre-2005 primary literature for experiments that reported molecular calcium-binding behavior of cardiac troponin C, rather than force generation alone. Using the evidence that was accessible and verifiable from archival abstracts and publisher snippets, I found **13 primary papers** that fit the topic and date window strongly enough to include, but **only 9** exposed enough numerical detail in the accessible record to standardize into useful affinity rows. I did **not** backfill missing primary values from later reviews or modeling tables, even when that would have made the spreadsheet prettier, because that would blur the line between primary extraction and secondary synthesis. Search exhausted under those constraints. citeturn13search0turn12search0turn12search2turn31search0turn23search2turn39search0

Where papers reported **association constants** \(K\_Ca\), I placed them in **Kd (M^-1)** as requested and inverted them to get **K (uM)**. Where papers reported **pCa50/K1/2**, I converted to dissociation constant only when the absolute pCa value itself was available in the accessible source. When a paper clearly qualified but the accessible record did not expose temperature, magnesium, or the exact fitted number, I left those cells blank rather than inventing them. citeturn12search0turn20search0turn24search0turn28search0turn23search2turn39search0

## Foundational isolated and skinned-system studies

**Citation.** Leavis, P. C., & Kraft, E. L. (1978). *Calcium binding to cardiac troponin C*. *Archives of Biochemistry and Biophysics, 186*(2), 411–415.  
**Relevance.** This is one of the earliest direct cardiac troponin C binding papers and clearly studies isolated cTnC with direct biophysical readouts. It is foundational because it establishes the now-familiar pattern of **two higher-affinity sites** and **one lower-affinity site** in cardiac TnC, with Mg²⁺ competition at the higher-affinity sites.  
**Methodology note.** Fluorescence, circular dichroism, and Ca²⁺-selective electrode measurements.  
**Flags/Caveats.** The accessible archival abstract did **not** expose the fitted binding constants, only the site-class conclusion; therefore the extraction below is intentionally partial. citeturn12search4turn13search0turn13search2

## Complex-context and modulatory studies

**Citation.** Putkey, J. A., Liu, W., Lin, X., Ahmed, S., Zhang, M., Potter, J. D., & Kerrick, W. G. L. (1997). *Fluorescent probes attached to Cys 35 or Cys 84 in cardiac troponin C are differentially sensitive to Ca2+-dependent events in vitro and in situ*. *Biochemistry, 36*(4), 970–978.  
**Relevance.** This is one of the richest pre-2005 datasets because it reports separate **pCa50 values** for free cTnC, binary complexes, ternary troponin, and reconstituted skinned fibers. It is exactly the kind of environment-sensitive affinity paper that matters for comparing isolated protein with more physiological assemblies.  
**Methodology note.** IAANS fluorescence titrations on recombinant cTnC derivatives, binary/ternary complexes, and skinned fibers.  
**Flags/Caveats.** Protein species is **recombinant chicken cTnC**; the in situ reconstitution used **rat cardiac fibers**. For the free-protein in vitro titrations, Mg²⁺ was omitted explicitly; for binary/ternary complexes the source states **5 mM MgCl₂**; for skinned fibers the standard solution contained **1 mM Mg²⁺**. ## Excluded and borderline papers

Several pre-2005 papers looked promising but I rejected them from the included set because they failed the user’s rule that the paper must provide a molecular cTnC calcium-binding metric, rather than merely structure, drug binding, or force-related behavior.

**Li, Spyracopoulos, Beier, Putkey, & Sykes (2000),** *Interaction of Cardiac Troponin C with Ca2+ Sensitizer EMD 57033 and Cardiac Troponin I Inhibitory Peptide* — rejected because the accessible quantitative results are **drug and peptide binding affinities**, and the paper explicitly concludes that the drug may act **“by not directly enhancing Ca²⁺ binding”** to the regulatory site. citeturn26search0turn26search1

**Wang, Li, & Sykes (2002),** *Structure of the regulatory N-domain of human cardiac troponin C in complex with human cardiac troponin I147-163 and bepridil* — rejected because the accessible abstract discusses structural consequences of Ca²⁺-bound cNTnC and drug/peptide interaction, but not a primary Ca²⁺ affinity value. citeturn36search3

**Takahashi-Yanaga et al. (2001),** *Functional Consequences of the Mutations in Human Cardiac Troponin I Gene Found in Familial Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy* — borderline. ## Coverage assessment and statistics

**Total included primary papers:** 13.  
**Papers with robust numerical extraction:** 9.  
**Papers included but only partially extractable from accessible records:** 4.  
**Total data rows extracted:** 24.  
**Search exhausted:** yes, under the user’s date cutoff and the stricter rule that every extracted value had to remain anchored to a primary pre-2005 source snippet rather than a later summary table.

The strongest coverage is for:  
- **isolated regulatory-site measurements** in the late 1990s to 2004,  
- **environmental shifts** between isolated cTnC, binary/ternary complexes, and skinned fibers, and  
- **species / temperature effects** in bovine versus salmonid systems. citeturn31search0turn20search0turn28search0turn23search2turn39search0

The weakest coverage is for:  
- explicit **Mg²⁺ buffer concentrations** in many older abstracts,  
- exact **absolute constants** in some early and peptide-modulation papers where only qualitative or threshold statements were exposed in the accessible snippet, and  
- clean separation of **site II versus sites III/IV** in the oldest studies. Those gaps are real literature-access gaps here, not places where I thought making up a number would be character-building. citeturn12search4turn15search4turn15search1turn15search0
