Mapping the Physiological Landscape of Cardiac Troponin C: A Pre-2005 Kinetic Analysis Across Protein States and Environmental Conditions
This report presents a comprehensive analysis of the primary scientific literature concerning the calcium-binding kinetics of Cardiac Troponin C (cTnC), adhering to a strict publication date cutoff of January 1, 2005. The objective is to systematically identify, extract, and standardize quantitative binding data from qualified research articles. This analysis covers studies employing various experimental techniques, including equilibrium dialysis, flow dialysis, and spectroscopic titrations. The extracted data are organized based on critical variables such as species origin, experimental temperature, magnesium ion concentration, and the macromolecular state of the protein, ranging from isolated cTnC to its context within reconstituted thin filaments. Each value has been converted and presented as either a dissociation constant (Kd) in micromolar (µM) or an association constant (Ka) in inverse molarity (M⁻¹), following the user-defined protocol. This work represents a foundational resource detailing the biophysical properties of this critical cardiac regulatory protein, highlighting methodological advancements and the profound influence of its physiological environment on its function.

Influence of Protein-Protein Interactions on Calcium Affinity
The functional role of Cardiac Troponin C (cTnC) in regulating cardiac muscle contraction is critically dependent on its integration into the larger troponin complex. The affinity of cTnC for calcium is profoundly modulated by its interactions with its partner proteins, Troponin I (cTnI) and Troponin T (cTnT). Studies conducted before 2005 consistently demonstrated that transitioning from the isolated cTnC monomer to the binary cTnC-cTnI complex and finally to the ternary troponin complex results in significant and functionally relevant changes in calcium binding affinity and kinetics. These findings underscore the principle of allostery, where the binding of a ligand (calcium) at one site influences the properties of another site, often across subunit interfaces.
The formation of the binary complex between cTnC and cTnI is a pivotal step in the activation mechanism. The unique N-terminal extension of cTnI, known as the switch peptide, interacts with the hydrophobic cleft of the N-domain of cTnC, particularly upon calcium binding

Studies published before 2005 provided substantial evidence that both of these physicochemical parameters significantly modulate the strength and kinetics of calcium binding to cTnC, with implications for cardiac performance under varying physiological and pathological conditions. Understanding these dependencies is essential for interpreting kinetic data and relating in vitro measurements to in vivo function, particularly during fever, hypothermia, or ischemic events characterized by acidosis.

The comprehensive analysis of cTnC's biophysical properties requires acknowledging that its function is not static but is finely tuned by the prevailing thermal and pH conditions of the cardiomyocyte.
