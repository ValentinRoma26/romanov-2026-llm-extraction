Impact of Protein State on Calcium-Binding Parameters
The calcium-binding affinity of Cardiac Troponin C (cTnC) is profoundly influenced by its structural context within the larger troponin complex and the actin-based thin filament. Reporting the affinity of isolated cTnC is informative for characterizing the intrinsic properties of the protein, but it does not reflect its functional behavior in the physiological setting. The pre-2005 literature provides ample evidence that the protein state—ranging from isolated monomer to components of the intact sarcomere—is a paramount variable that dictates cTnC's calcium sensitivity. At the simplest level is isolated cTnC, typically the N-domain responsible for regulatory functions 
pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

However, the true regulatory power of cTnC emerges upon its integration into the ternary troponin complex (cTnC-cTnI-cTnT) and subsequently into the thin filament. The interaction with Troponin I (cTnI) is particularly critical, as it introduces an allosteric regulatory layer. In the absence of calcium, cTnI binds to and inhibits the interaction between actin and myosin.
