Introduction
Cardiac troponin C (cTnC) is the calcium-sensing component of the cardiac muscle thin filament, playing a pivotal role in the regulation of muscle contraction and relaxation through its calcium-binding properties. Understanding the quantitative metrics of calcium binding to cTnC—such as dissociation constants (KdK_dKd​), association constants (KaK_aKa​), and pCa50pCa_{50}pCa50​ values—under various physiological conditions is essential for elucidating the molecular mechanisms of cardiac function and disease. This report presents a systematic and comprehensive literature search focused on primary research papers published before January 1, 2005, that report experimental data on the calcium-binding properties of cTnC. The goal is to identify up to 20 distinct primary research articles meeting strict inclusion criteria and to extract quantitative binding metrics under varying experimental conditions.

Methodology
The literature search was conducted across multiple databases including PubMed, Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, Web of Science, and Scopus. Search terms were precisely constructed to target primary experimental studies reporting calcium-binding metrics for cTnC, such as "cardiac troponin C calcium binding kinetics," "cTnC affinity," and "calcium dissociation constant cTnC." The inclusion criteria were strictly applied: papers must be primary experimental research published before 2005, reporting quantitative calcium-binding metrics derived from direct binding assays or spectroscopic titrations, and must involve cardiac troponin C from vertebrate species. Exclusion criteria eliminated reviews, computational models, and papers lacking quantitative binding data or using invalid methodologies.

Relevance: This study directly addresses the kinetics of calcium binding to the regulatory site of cardiac troponin C using a monocysteine mutant labeled with a fluorescent probe. It provides a detailed kinetic description of the Ca²⁺ activation cycle of isolated cTnC, revealing rapid binding and release at site II accompanied by slow conformational changes. Relevance: This study investigates calcium and cardiac troponin I (cTnI) peptide binding to human cTnC using NMR spectroscopy. Discussion
The comprehensive literature search identified only four primary research papers published before 2005 that met the strict inclusion criteria for experimental calcium-binding data on cardiac troponin C. These papers provide valuable insights into the kinetics and thermodynamics of calcium binding to cTnC but do not explicitly report the quantitative binding metrics (KdK_dKd​, KaK_aKa​, pCa50pCa_{50}pCa50​) in the accessible snippets. The full texts of these papers are required to extract the precise quantitative data needed for a complete analysis.
The experimental methods employed in these studies include fluorescence spectroscopy with labeled mutants, NMR spectroscopy, and physiological measurements, offering a range of approaches to characterize calcium binding. The studies cover different species (human, bovine), temperatures (e.g., 30 °C), and troponin complex states (isolated cTnC, binary complexes), which are essential variables for understanding the binding properties under varying conditions.
The exclusion of numerous papers due to lack of quantitative data, invalid methodologies, or publication dates beyond the cutoff highlights the challenges in accessing relevant pre-2005 literature. This suggests that either the search strategy needs to be expanded or alternative methods for accessing these papers should be explored to gather a more comprehensive dataset.

Conclusion
The search for primary research papers published before 2005 on the calcium-binding properties of cardiac troponin C (cTnC) has yielded limited results. Only four papers were identified as potentially relevant, but the full texts could not be accessed to extract the necessary quantitative binding metrics. The snippets from these papers suggest that they contain valuable information on the kinetics of calcium binding to cTnC, but without access to the full texts, the extraction of specific data such as KdK_dKd​, KaK_aKa​, and pCa50pCa_{50}pCa50​ is not possible. Therefore, the search for accessible and relevant papers needs to be expanded or alternative methods for accessing these papers should be explored.
